Organising the living space with the existing yang openings

A step-by-step guide · Feng Shui Research Center · Master Eduardo Hess

Every room already has yang openings: the entrance door of the house, the doors of the rooms and the windows that can be opened. Seen from a facing point — the head of the bed, the centre of a door, the desk — each opening falls on one hexagram. The aim is to place the bed, the desk, the doors and the items so that these existing openings fall on the good Ling Shen water hexagrams of that facing.

The order is always the same: first try to reach the good hexagrams with the openings that are already there, moving the furniture; only for what cannot be reached add a fountain.

  • A window counts only if it can be opened. Fixed glass is not a yang opening.
  • When two openings ask for different moves, there is no automatic priority: the app shows the options and you decide case by case.

Part 1 — The procedure

  1. Facing point. The centre of the bed head, the centre of the door, or the desk. The facing is the Zheng Shen (ZS) of that piece (for the bed: sitting + 180°).
  2. Good water positions. They are the candidates of 📷 Photo sectors: Ling Shen hexagrams within ±70° of the facing, linked with the facing and with the people.
  3. Find. From the facing point take a wide photo straight ahead (0.5×): you see roughly where the openings fall.
  4. Final check. From the same point, take a 1× photo turned toward each opening. Type its direction in Photo centre, then 🧱 Align to a wall on a wall running away from you — the direction is where the wall RUNS (for example 266°), not where it faces. Use the magnifier to place the points. The edges of a wide photo can be off by 2–3°.
  5. Laser. Measure the horizontal distance from the facing point to the centre of each opening: door closed; window closed, aiming at the central mullion.
  6. Calculate. With the bearing θ and the distance D of an opening, and the facing φ:
    • forward F = D·cos(θ − φ), lateral L = D·sin(θ − φ) (positive = to the right, looking along the facing);
    • moving the piece s to the right and t forward gives the new bearing φ + atan2(L − s, F − t).
    Two openings with sideways and forward moves give two equations: there is an exact solution that centres both. With sideways moves only there is often no common solution: the app shows one option per opening, with a boundary warning where a centre ends up near the edge of a hexagram.
  7. Width. The angular width of an opening, edge to edge, against the 5.625° of a hexagram: it shows whether the whole opening can fit in one hexagram. The closer you are to an opening, the wider it looks.
  8. Fountain. What no opening reaches → a fountain on the chosen Ling Shen hexagram, nozzle inside the box (checked on a 1× photo), switched daily by the fountain rule (water hexagram ↔ hour ↔ day).
  9. Save each calibrated picture: 📦 Save to Dropbox → House profiles / <house> / <facing> facing.

Part 2 — The 🪟 Yang openings tool

Where it is: Feng Shui → 🏠 HOUSE PROFILES → the house card → ☰ → 🪟 Yang openings.

1. Choose the facing source

The list is the same as in Photo sectors: House · Doors · Beds · Desks · Items of the active floor. Under it you see the good Ling Shen water hexagrams of that facing, with their degrees.

2. The openings

The doors come from the house card (external and internal; items are not openings; a door is not listed when it is itself the facing source). Add the windows with + 🪟 Window: the app asks whether the window can be opened — fixed glass is not saved.

For each opening type:

  • Centre — the bearing of its centre from the facing point;
  • Distance — the laser distance to its centre, in metres;
  • Edges (optional) — the bearings of its left and right edge, for the width.

The fastest way is from 📷 Photo sectors: choose the same facing source, calibrate the photo, tap 📍 Measure a point on the opening, then 🪟 Save as opening. The app asks which opening, whether the point is the centre or an edge, and the laser distance.

MarkMeaning
✓ hex NThe centre is on a good Ling Shen hexagram of this facing.
⚠ hex NGood hexagram, but the centre is closer than the boundary value (default 1°) to its edge. You can change the value at the bottom of the list.
✗ hex NNot a good hexagram: Zheng Shen, Ling Shen beyond ±70°, or Ling Shen not linked with the facing.

3. 📐 Move calculator

  1. For each measured opening choose its target hexagram (or "not a target"). The app proposes the good hexagram the opening is on, or the nearest one.
  2. Say how the piece may move: sideways (max cm) and/or forward / back (max cm). Left, right and forward are seen from the facing point looking along the facing (for a bed: from the head toward the foot).
  3. Calculate. Every option shows the move and where every opening falls after it:
    • Smallest move that puts all the targets on their hexagram, at least the boundary value from the edges;
    • Best centred — the largest distance from the edges;
    • Exact centring of two openings, when sideways and forward moves are both allowed;
    • when no move satisfies all the targets: one option per opening.
  4. Tap Choose on the option you decide. The choice is saved with the facing source.

4. ⛲ Good hexagrams still without an opening

After the chosen move (or now, if nothing is chosen) the app lists the good hexagrams that no opening reaches. For each one: 🕐 Daily hours (today and the next two days, the hours that pass the fountain rule) and 🔎 Dates to place it (opens the Water section, with this facing and this water degree on the luopan).

Part 4 — Photo and laser: how to measure

Bearing (photo)

  1. Open 📷 Photo sectors from the house card and choose the same facing source (e.g. the bed).
  2. Stand or sit at the facing point. Phone upright and level.
  3. Wide photo straight ahead (0.5×) to see where the openings fall.
  4. For each opening: a 1× photo from the same point, turned toward it, the opening near the middle of the picture. Type the direction of the photo in Photo centre.
  5. 🧱 Align to a wall: trace two horizontal edges (skirting board, ceiling line) of a wall that runs away from you; answer with the direction where the wall RUNS (the house facing or ±90° / 180°). The magnifier follows your finger; the point is placed where you lift it.
  6. 📍 Measure a point on the centre of the opening (and, if you want the width, on its two edges) → 🪟 Save as opening.
Near the edges of a wide photo the boxes can be off by 2–3° — half a hexagram. The bearings you save must come from the 1× photo turned toward the opening, calibrated on a wall.

Distance (laser)

  • From the facing point (bed head centre, door centre, desk) to the centre of the opening, horizontal.
  • Door closed: aim at the middle of the door leaf.
  • Window closed: aim at the central mullion.

Save the pictures

📦 Save to Dropbox stores each calibrated picture in House profiles / <house> / <facing> facing, so the measurements can be checked later.

Part 5 — When to add a fountain

  • Only for a good Ling Shen hexagram that no existing opening reaches with the move you chose.
  • Place it with Photo sectors: the nozzle (where the water moves) inside the red box, checked on a 1× photo turned toward it.
  • Switch it on every day by the fountain rule: the water hexagram must communicate with the HOUR, and the hour with the DAY (or be Sheng In / Ke In to it). In the tool, 🕐 Daily hours lists the hours that pass the rule, each with its links.
  • 🔎 Dates to place it opens the Water section, with this facing and this water degree on the luopan, to choose the date of placement.

Part 6 — Asking the AI assistant

💬 Examples:

  • "Yang openings of the bed in Vienna: where do the door and the window fall?"
  • "The bedroom window is at 222.9°, 3.98 m from the bed head — save it."
  • "How much do I move the bed, sideways only, max 60 cm, to put the door on 62 and the window on 46?"

The assistant reads and saves the measurements of the tool and runs the move calculator. When the openings disagree it lists every option with the hexagram of every opening; the choice stays yours (Choose in the 🪟 Yang openings panel).
