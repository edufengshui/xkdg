XKDG App — Photo Sectors & Floor Plan

A step-by-step guide · Feng Shui Research Center · Master Eduardo Hess

This guide covers three tools of the XKDG app:

  1. Water positions on a photo: take a photo from the facing point of a door or a bed, and the app draws on it the red boxes where the good Ling Shen water hexagrams fall.
  2. Palaces and mountains on photos from the centre: four photos from the centre of the house, with the 8 directions and the 24 mountains drawn on the room.
  3. Floor plan builder: build the floor plan from laser measurements, with the 8 palaces drawn automatically.

Before you start

  • Update the app: close it completely and open it again.
  • Save your house in Feng Shui → House Profiles, with its facing (degrees) and period. Add the doors you use, each with its own facing.
  • Save the people who live there, with birth date and time. The water analysis checks them: first by the year pillar, then by the day pillar (month and hour do not count).
  • A phone with a wide lens (0.5×) is best: it shows more of the room.
  • For the floor plan you need a laser distance meter.
The one reliable direction is your house facing. Indoor compass readings drift by several degrees because of metal, wiring and appliances. The app therefore calibrates every photo on the walls: every wall of the house runs along the facing or at 90° / 180° from it.

Part 1 — Water positions on a photo (from a door or a bed)

Use this when you want to place a fountain, an aquarium or a water basin for a door or for the bed (or a desk). The photo is taken from the point that "faces" (Zheng Shen): the centre of the door, or the head of the bed.

1. Take the photo

  1. Stand (or sit) exactly at the facing point: the centre of the door, or the head of the bed, looking out the way the door or the bed faces.
  2. Hold the phone upright and level, not tilted up or down, and shoot straight ahead.
  3. Use the wide lens (0.5×) if you have one. Do not crop or edit the photo.

2. Open Photo sectors

  1. Go to Feng Shui → House Profiles and find your house card.
  2. Tap the house ☰, then 📷 Photo sectors.
  3. In Facing, choose the facing you are working on: the list is grouped in House, Doors, Beds, Desks and Items (all saved in the house; for a bed the facing is its sitting + 180°), or type it under custom…. The app shows the facing hexagram and the people it will check.
  4. Tap 📷 Photo and take or choose the photo.

3. Check the lens

The app reads the lens from the photo data when it can (it says "from photo data"). If it says "lens unknown", choose the lens you used in Lens (e.g. wide 0.5× · 13 mm (iPhone)), or type its 35 mm-equivalent focal length.

4. Calibrate on a wall (important)

  1. Tap 🧱 Align to a wall.
  2. Pick a wall that runs away from you in the photo (a side wall, not the one in front of you).
  3. Tap 2 points along one horizontal edge of that wall, for example the skirting board.
  4. Tap 2 points along another horizontal edge of the same wall, for example where it meets the ceiling.
  5. The app asks "This wall runs toward which direction?" and lists the four wall directions (the house facing, ±90°, 180°), marking the most likely one. Check that it is really the direction of that wall and confirm its number.
  6. The blue line under the buttons now says "Aligned to a wall … centre fixed". The photo is calibrated.
If something looks wrong, tap Clear references and trace the wall again, choosing longer and straighter edges. Two walls at 90° (one on each side) make it even more precise.

5. Read the water positions

  1. Under the photo, the list Water positions shows the Ling Shen hexagrams within ±70° of the facing that communicate with the facing hexagram (period, family) and with the people (✓ year, ~ day).
  2. Tick a position to draw its red box on the photo. Each box shows the hexagram number and its degree.
  3. Use the Boxes top / bottom sliders to move the boxes up or down to the height of your furniture.
  4. Choose the box that falls on a usable spot (no window, door or furniture in the way).

6. Check the exact spot

  1. Tap 📍 Measure a point and tap on the photo where the water will stand.
  2. A yellow line appears with the degree and the hexagram of that point (and Pure Yin / Pure Yang when it applies). Check that the whole object stands inside the red box, not on its edge; if it is close to an edge, move it towards the centre of the box.
  3. Tap ⬇ Save / share image to keep the photo with the boxes.
With the assistant (💬): you can also say "Help me place a fountain for the bed" (or "for the main door"). The assistant leads a short interview: house, facing, people, best positions, which spots are usable in the room, a photo check, and finally the dates to activate the water. You can attach the photo directly in the chat (📷).
Optional: calibrate with a compass instead of a wall
  1. From the photo spot, read the facing with your compass and tap Set it from the facing: the app computes the compass correction (the indoor error).
  2. Tap 🎯 Point read with the compass, tap a point in the photo and type the compass reading of that point. The correction is applied automatically.

Part 2 — The 8 palaces and 24 mountains on photos from the centre

About 2 min, no sound: the captions explain every step (Part 2 of this guide).

This shows how the space around you is divided into the 8 directions (palaces, with their trigrams) and the 24 mountains, drawn directly on the photos of the room.

The photos must be taken from the CENTRE of the house — the whole house, not a single room. The direction lines are only correct from that point.

1. Take four photos from the centre

  1. Stand at the centre of the house. Hold the phone upright and level, wide lens (0.5×).
  2. Take four photos, one towards each wall: towards the facing, then turn 90° and shoot again, and so on, so the four photos cover the whole space.
  3. Note which wall each photo faces, e.g. photo 1 = facing 175°, photo 2 = 265°, photo 3 = 355°, photo 4 = 85° (facing, +90°, +180°, +270°).

2. Open each photo in Photo sectors

  1. House Profiles → your house → 📷 Photo sectors → 📷 Photo → choose the first photo.
  2. In Photo centre, type the approximate direction of that photo (from your note in step 1). The app then proposes the right wall direction when you calibrate.
  3. Calibrate with 🧱 Align to a wall, as in Part 1, step 4, and confirm the direction of the wall you traced.
  4. Under Show, tick 8 directions and/or 24 mountains. You can untick water hexagrams to keep the picture clean.
  5. Tap ⬇ Save / share image.
  6. Repeat for the other three photos.
The four saved images together show the whole space divided into the palaces and mountains. You can see at once where furniture, doors and water fall.

Part 3 — Floor plan from laser measurements

Every wall of the house follows the facing or its perpendicular. Each room is therefore a rectangle that is already oriented: two measurements per room are enough. The assistant asks the questions, the app draws the plan.

1. Prepare

  • A laser distance meter. Hold its back edge against one wall and aim square at the opposite wall.
  • The house saved with its facing (House Profiles).

2. Build it with the assistant

  1. Open the assistant (💬), area Feng Shui, and write: "Help me build the floor plan of my house".
  2. It asks one measurement at a time, in this order:
    1. the internal wall thickness: measure it once on a door frame;
    2. the first room, usually the entrance: its two sizes, named by the wall directions (e.g. "from the wall towards 175° to the opposite wall");
    3. each next room: which room it touches and on which side, how far along that wall it starts, and its two sizes.
  3. Every 2–3 rooms the assistant shows the drawing. Check it and tell it what to correct.
  4. An L-shaped room is entered as two rectangles joined together.
  5. When all rooms are in, the assistant opens the plan in the floor-plan tool with the 8 palaces drawn from the centre. Tap Save to house there to keep the picture with the stars.

3. See or edit the plan

  • In the house card, 📏 Plan builder shows the drawing and the list of rooms, with their sizes along and across the facing.
  • You can delete a room there, or ask the assistant to change it.
  • 🖼 Use as floor plan (8 palaces) sends the plan to the floor-plan tool.
  • If the app warns "The centre falls outside the rooms", the plan is irregular: check the missing sectors.

Part 4 — Furniture and ZS / LS optimisation

Once the rooms are in the plan, the assistant can add the doors and the furniture and check, for example, the bed against the room door.

  1. Open the assistant (💬, area 🏠 Feng Shui) and say "let's add the furniture of the bedroom" or "check my bed against the door".
  2. It asks, one at a time: on which wall each door is (by direction), the laser distance from the corner it names to the jamb, and the clear width of the doorway.
  3. Then each piece of furniture: the wall its back touches (for the bed, the headboard wall), the distance from the same corner, its width and its depth. A photo of the room (📷) helps it recognise the objects, but the numbers always come from the laser.
  4. The bed's sitting is the direction of the headboard wall: it must be Zheng Shen (or period 1/9).
  5. From the centre of the headboard, the centre of the door opening must fall on a Ling Shen hexagram, within ±70° of the bed facing, that communicates with the facing hexagram.
  6. The assistant slides the bed along its wall centimetre by centimetre and tells you, for example, "move the bed 35 cm toward 84° (E)". On the drawing (📏 Plan builder) a dashed line goes from the bed to the door: green = good, red = not yet.

3D view

House card ☰ → 🧊 3D view (or the button in 📏 Plan builder, or ask the assistant "show me the plan in 3D"): rooms, walls with doors and windows, the furniture as real shapes, the 8 palaces drawn on the floor from the centre, the red facing arrow and the last bed–door line. One finger rotates, two fingers zoom; ⬇ Top looks from above, 🧱 Low walls cuts the walls so you see inside. For true heights the assistant asks the ceiling height once (and, if you want, door and window heights). It needs the internet the first time it opens.

Furniture in the real photo of the room

House card ☰ → 📷 Room photo + furniture (or tell the assistant "put the table from this photo in the living room" and attach the photo of the shop tag): the furniture of the plan is drawn into your real photo, true size and true perspective. A piece you are only considering is marked in prova and drawn semi-transparent.

  1. Take the photo standing still, phone upright and level (wide lens is best), showing two corners or a door. Load it.
  2. Type where you stood: the laser distances to two walls (by direction) and the phone height (about 1.5 m).
  3. Calibrate: choose a known point (a room corner, a door edge), tap it in the photo; then a second one on the other side.
  4. Check: the cyan lines (floor edges and corners) and the yellow door frames must sit on the real ones. Fine-tune with turn / tilt / height.
  5. Choose the piece and move it 5 cm at a time; 📥 Save image keeps the picture. Photos stay on this device (not in the backup).
One hexagram is 5.6° wide — about 30 cm at 3 m. Measure carefully, to the centimetre.
